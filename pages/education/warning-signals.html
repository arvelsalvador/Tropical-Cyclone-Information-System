Typhoon Warning Signals

PAGASA warning signals show expected wind strength and the action your family should take as a typhoon approaches.

How Signals Work

How the signals work

Signals are based on expected winds, not current rain. Check official updates and act early when a signal is raised.

The Five Warning Signals

1 39–61 km/h May be raised ~36 hrs before impact

Winds of 39–61 km/h within 36 hours

Impacts

  • Twigs of small trees may sway and break
  • Light damage to old, dilapidated structures

Action

  • Monitor PAGASA bulletins and local advisories
  • Check your emergency go-bag and supplies
2 62–88 km/h May be raised ~24 hrs before impact

Winds of 62–88 km/h within 24 hours

Impacts

  • Some coconut and banana trees may be tilted or broken
  • Light to moderate damage to old roofs and houses

Action

  • Stay indoors and away from windows
  • Reinforce doors, windows, and roofing
3 89–117 km/h May be raised ~18 hrs before impact

Winds of 89–117 km/h within 18 hours

Impacts

  • Heavy damage to agriculture; many trees downed
  • Moderate to heavy damage to old and substandard structures
  • Power lines may be toppled; outages likely
  • Sea travel hazardous for most vessels

Action

  • Evacuate early if you live in a flood- or landslide-prone area
  • Charge phones and power banks fully
  • Store drinking water in clean containers
  • Keep family members within one safe location
4 118–184 km/h May be raised ~12 hrs before impact

Winds of 118–184 km/h within 12 hours

Impacts

  • Severe damage to houses, buildings, and infrastructure
  • Coconut plantations may be largely destroyed
  • Widespread power and communication outages
  • Very rough seas; all sea travel extremely dangerous

Action

  • Evacuate immediately to the nearest safe shelter
  • Turn off electricity and LPG if flooding is likely
  • Bring your go-bag, documents, and water
  • Stay away from rivers, coasts, and steep slopes
5 185+ km/h May be raised ~3 hrs before impact

Winds of 185 km/h or higher within 3 hours

Impacts

  • Widespread, near-total devastation of structures
  • Extreme wind damage even to well-built buildings
  • Debris-filled winds make outdoor movement deadly
  • Long-lasting loss of utilities and access routes

Action

  • Shelter in the strongest part of an evacuation center
  • If sheltering at home, stay in an interior room on the lowest floor (above flood level)
  • Do not leave shelter until officials declare it safe
  • Watch out for the calm "eye" — winds return suddenly

Quick Reference

Signal Wind Speed Lead Time Key Action
No. 1 39–61 km/h ~36 hrs before impact Monitor and prepare
No. 2 62–88 km/h ~24 hrs before impact Stay indoors, secure home
No. 3 89–117 km/h ~18 hrs before impact Evacuate if at risk
No. 4 118–184 km/h ~12 hrs before impact Evacuate immediately
No. 5 185+ km/h ~3 hrs before impact Shelter in strongest structure

Wind ranges and lead times follow PAGASA's revised TCWS guidelines (2022). Always follow the latest official bulletin for your area.

Important Reminders

Signals are about wind, not rain

Heavy flooding can happen even at Signal No. 1. Watch rainfall warnings too.

Signals change — keep updated

Signals can be upgraded or downgraded as the storm moves. Check bulletins every few hours.

Act early, not when it's strong

The safest evacuation happens before Signal No. 3. Don't wait for the worst.

Follow local officials

Preemptive evacuation orders from your barangay override everything else. Comply promptly.

Know the signal? Know your route.

Pair your signal knowledge with a ready evacuation plan and a packed go-bag.
