Evacuation Guide

When a typhoon threatens, the safest decision is the one made early. This guide walks you through when to leave, where to go, and what to bring — so your household can move quickly and calmly if an evacuation order is issued.

When to Evacuate

The golden rule: leave early

Roads flood quickly and visibility drops fast. Evacuating while conditions are still manageable takes under an hour — evacuating at the height of a storm can be impossible. If you live in a flood-prone, landslide-prone, or coastal area, or your home is made of light materials, treat evacuation as your default plan, not a last resort.

When Should You Evacuate?

Match your situation to the current PAGASA warning signal. Not sure what each signal means? Read the Warning Signals guide.

Signal No. 1–2

Prepare and monitor — no need to leave yet

  • Check your go-bag, water, and food supplies
  • Charge phones, power banks, and flashlights
  • Secure windows, roofing, and outdoor items
  • Identify your nearest evacuation center and route
  • Agree on a family meeting point and communication plan
Signal No. 3

Evacuate now if you are in a risky area

  • Leave immediately if you are near rivers, coasts, or slopes
  • Houses with light materials should consider moving early
  • Move valuables and documents to waterproof bags, high up
  • Expect power interruptions — top off all batteries
  • Keep listening to official bulletins every hour
Signal No. 4–5

Everyone in at-risk areas must be in a safe shelter

  • Leave before conditions worsen — do not wait for landfall
  • Turn off electricity and LPG at the main switch if leaving
  • Bring the go-bag, water, and important documents only
  • Never attempt to cross flooded roads or swollen rivers
  • If travel is no longer safe, shelter in the strongest interior room
Any signal

A preemptive evacuation order always wins

  • When your barangay or LGU orders evacuation, comply immediately
  • Officials know local flood history better than any forecast
  • Bring pets only if the center explicitly allows animals
  • Help neighbors who are elderly, disabled, or living alone
  • Register with center staff so family can find you

Critical Reminders

15 cm of moving water can knock you down

Turn around, don't drown. Never walk or drive through floodwater — it hides open manholes and debris.

Know two routes, not one

Floods can block your primary route. Agree on a backup route and a meeting point in case family members are separated.

Plan for those who need help

Assign one adult per senior, child, or person with a disability. Include their medicines and special needs in the go-bag.

Practice the plan before the storm

Walk the route with your family during fair weather. A plan you have rehearsed takes half the time when it matters.

Route ready? Bag packed? You're prepared.

Round out your plan with signal knowledge and everyday preparedness habits.
