Preparedness Tips

Simple steps to help you stay safe before, during, and after a typhoon.

View the Guide

Start with a plan

Prepare your household, check official warnings, and keep essential supplies within reach.

Your preparedness guide

Follow these practical steps at every stage of the storm.

Prepare Year-round habit

What to do before a typhoon is forecast

House & home

  • Clear gutters, drains, and downspouts of leaves and debris
  • Trim weak tree branches that could fall on your roof
  • Reinforce windows with plywood or storm shutters
  • Check roofing for loose sheets and secure with nails or bolts
  • Elevate items off the floor if you're in a flood-prone area

Supplies & planning

  • Build and maintain an emergency go-bag (see checklist below)
  • Stock at least 3 days of drinking water per household member
  • Map two evacuation routes to your nearest safe shelter
  • Agree on a family meeting point and out-of-area contact
  • Keep copies of important documents in a waterproof pouch
During When storm is near

What to do while the typhoon is approaching or passing

Stay safe indoors

  • Stay away from windows and glass doors
  • Move to an interior room on the lowest floor if winds are strong
  • Never go outside during the calm "eye" — winds return from the other side
  • Keep a flashlight and radio on in case of power outages

Communication & monitoring

  • Listen to PAGASA bulletins and local radio for updates
  • Keep phones charged; use power banks wisely
  • Send check-in messages to your out-of-area contact
  • Help neighbors — especially elderly or those living alone
After Recovery phase

What to do after the typhoon passes

Check & assess

  • Wait for the official all-clear before leaving shelter
  • Check for gas leaks, damaged wires, and structural damage before re-entering
  • Report downed power lines to the utility company — never touch them
  • Boil or purify tap water before drinking until the supply is declared safe

Recover & restock

  • Photograph damage for insurance claims before cleaning up
  • Throw away food that came in contact with floodwater
  • Restock your emergency kit — you may need it again soon
  • Check on neighbors and community members who may need help

Quick Preparedness Tips

Start early, not when it's coming

The best time to prepare is during fair weather. Don't wait for a typhoon warning to stock up.

Water is life — store enough

At least 3 liters per person per day, for at least 3 days. After a typhoon, tap water may not be safe.

Know your evacuation route

Map two routes to your nearest shelter. Walk them once before typhoon season so they're familiar.

Practice your family plan

Run a family drill before typhoon season. A plan you've rehearsed takes half the time when it matters.

Keep cash on hand

ATMs and card machines won't work without power. Keep small bills and loose change in your kit.

Look out for each other

Check on elderly neighbors, those with disabilities, and families with young children before and after the storm.

Kit packed? Plan practiced? You're ready.

Round out your preparedness with warning signal knowledge and a clear evacuation route.
